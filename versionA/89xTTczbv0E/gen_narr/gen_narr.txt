In the heart of Luxor, Egypt, Dr. Zbigniew Szybranski leads a team of archaeologists on a mission to uncover the secrets of the revolutionary female pharaoh Hatshepsut. As they restore her unique temple, John and Maria stumble upon an unfinished ram-headed Sphinx at a vast site, while Martina Bartanova's team prepares to open an unexplored tomb in a nearby necropolis. Their progress is abruptly halted by the discovery of a buried body, raising questions about ancient burial practices. Meanwhile, John and Maria's excavation at Gopel, El Sorsilla, reveals a rare miniature sphinx, all under the watchful eye of dig inspector Hollid Shockey. Amidst these discoveries, Martina investigates fragile human remains, shedding light on the roles of women in ancient Egyptian society. The team uncovers mummified body parts, including King Tut's detached penis, and a beautiful cartonage face mask, all while battling the elements. As they delve deeper, they reveal insights into Hatshepsut's rise to power, her conflict with her stepson Tutmose III, and her monumental building projects. The story of Hatshepsut's reign and the power dynamics of ancient Egypt come to life through these remarkable archaeological finds. How did Hatshepsut's decision to depict herself with male attributes influence her acceptance and authority as a female pharaoh in ancient Egyptian society, and what does this reveal about the gender dynamics and power structures of the time?
